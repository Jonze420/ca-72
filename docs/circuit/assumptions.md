# Assumptions, limitations and open questions

Each simplification says what behaviour it leaves out and when it is revisited. Open
questions about the drawings themselves are in the board documents (B1-..., and so on).

## Simplifications in the real-time models (against the circuit reference)

| # | Simplification | What it leaves out (measured against ngspice) | Revisit |
|---|---|---|---|
| A11 | The filter's gain recovery amplifier (Q8, Q6, Q7, Q5) is its DC transfer, tabulated from ngspice: its transistors' capacitances are left out | The Miller effect on Q7's base (about 50 ns of lag on half the signal): the resonance 0.9 cent sharp at 1.2 kHz, 1.4 cent at 3.5 kHz, 3-4 cents at 11 kHz; a +25 dB resonant peak at 11 kHz 2.2 dB lower; self-oscillation 1.1-3 cents sharp (`capacitance_budget`) | If the owner's comparison points at high, strongly resonant settings; the TIS97's capacitances are themselves typical values (B4-3) |
| A12 | The ladder's bias-dependent device effects (base currents, series resistance, high injection, junction and diffusion capacitance) are evaluated with each pair balanced, at the tail current; the pairs' base-collector voltage is taken as -0.9 V (the bias chain drops 1.47 V per stage) | Their variation over a large signal's swing (second order: they are all below 1 % of the ladder's currents or its C) | If the large-signal comparisons (drive, self-oscillation) ever disagree |
| A13 | The output stage's tables are at 25 C and the default bench's operating point (nt held at its resting voltage) | The output pair's temperature dependence | With A4 |
| A14 | The VCA pairs' base currents are linear in their tanh about rest (each side's resting current plus the small-signal slope), and their Early effect is left out except the output pair's | The output pair loads the second pair about 5 % less than in ngspice: the gain is 0.09 dB high; the higher harmonics of a hard-driven VCA differ by up to 1.3 dB | If the owner's comparison points at the VCA's level or overdrive |
| A15 | The VCA's balance is set with the contour at 5 V (the attack's end, service manual 2.10; board 2's loudness contour peaks at 5.28 V and sustains at up to about 4.9 V); the A-440 off (its feed at 0 V) while the balance is set | The 1st VCA balance's exact setting point | When the whole instrument's calibration is run on the model |
| A16 | The contour generators' +9.3 V rail is ideal at its resting value (Q26's decoupling is not modelled) | Its sag under the timing capacitors' charging current: about 75 mV with both attacks at their fastest, where the model's attack runs up to 70 mV ahead mid-attack | If the owner's comparison points at fast attacks |
| A17 | In the voice, the contour generators run at half the output rate (24 kHz at 48 kHz), linearly interpolated, and the filter contour reaches the control node and the loudness contour the VCA through that interpolation | Against ngspice at 24 kHz: up to 86 mV in the fastest attack (72 mV at 48 kHz) and trigger edges within 0.19 ms (0.15 ms) (`contour_realtime.rs`). At 12 kHz the fastest attack was 183 mV off | When the cost is measured (plan stage 7): 48 kHz if the contours are cheap enough, a faster attack model if not |
| A18 | The key contacts: 0.1 ohm each (gold spring on gold bar, with the wiring); a key's pitch contact closes with its trigger contact and opens 2 ms after it (the service manual requires only the order) | With two keys held the lower sounds 1.1 cents sharp at 0.1 ohm (0.5 at 0.05); a release with both contacts opening together holds the note about 6 cents sharp (the hold switch lets go 1.2 ms after the trigger contact) | If the owner's comparison points at legato pitch or the release's pitch |
| A19 | The keyboard circuit's load is resistors to fixed voltages: the three oscillators' keyboard inputs (51.1K each) to -5 V (their summing junctions, or for oscillator 3 with OSC. 3 CONTROL off the -5 V line), the filter's KEYBOARD CONTROL resistors to 0 V (its control node moves about +-0.8 V) | The summing junctions' microvolt offsets; the filter's control node's movement (a 300K load moving 0.8 V changes the keyboard's load current by 2.7 uA) | Not expected to matter: the amplifier's loop corrects the load at its output (a first version loading to 0 V moved the output by 0.4 mV at the lowest key) |
| A20 | The oscillators' core model (the reset's timing, the jump) is fitted to ngspice from 0.7 Hz to 4.2 kHz (`fit_core.py`) and used above it | Above 4.2 kHz it runs flat: -1.2 cents at 5.6 kHz (2' high A with a FREQUENCY control at 10) | When the owner's comparison or the benchmarks reach the top of the range: refit the core to about 8 kHz |
| A24 | The modulation line is the modulation mix amplifier's DC transfer behind its output resistance (measured sourcing 1 mA), with R30 alone once Q7 would have to sink | The transition between the two: 1.7 mV off ngspice while Q7 sources firmly, 7.5 mV and 17 mV as oscillator 3 nears +2.5 V with the wheel fully forward on the MOD bus (`modmix_realtime.rs`) | If the owner's comparison points at a strongly modulated tone's shape |
| A25 | The EXTERNAL INPUT jack's voltage is the side chain's sample times 5 V, the same scale as the output (so a render fed back to the input behaves as the instrument does) | The level a given source would have had at the jack | If the owner's use (line level, instruments) wants another default |

## Limits of validation

- Agreement with ngspice shows the real-time models agree with the transcribed circuit and
  these device models. It does not show agreement with a physical instrument.
- The owner's hardware reference is existing recordings of unknown revision and condition
  (decision 2026-09-28). Comparisons with them are qualitative.
